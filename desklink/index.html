DeskLink

Long message to write on your phone? Type it on your computer instead. DeskLink turns a browser tab on your PC into a keyboard, and optionally a mouse, for your Android phone. You don't install anything on the computer, and there's no cable or account. Away from the PC, it's a full phone keyboard with long-press accents, themes, swipe and voice typing. The PC page can also show the phone's screen, its notifications, and its storage in a full file explorer.

Personal project · Not publicly available yet

How it works

Start a session on the phone, open the address it shows in a browser on your PC, and scan the QR code with the phone. When both screens show the same 6-digit code, confirm on the phone, and you're typing.

A new phone keyboard

New in 0.5.0. Long-press keys for accents and symbols, two pages of symbols, an optional number row, a toolbar and menu, one-handed mode and three keyboard heights. The Enter key changes to Search, Send or Next to fit the field.

Themes for the keyboard

New in 0.5.0. System auto, colours from your wallpaper (Android 12+), Light, Dark, Black, solid colours, soft pastels and gradients, each with key borders on or off. The rest of the keyboard follows the theme.

A file explorer for the phone

New in 0.5.0. Browse the phone's storage from the PC with a folder tree, breadcrumbs, list, details or big-icon views and sorting. Make folders, rename, delete, cut, copy and paste, drag and drop, upload and download, and search by name.

View, play and edit

New in 0.5.0. Photo thumbnails and a viewer that rotates and flips pictures on the phone, video and audio playback in the page, and a text editor that saves straight back to the phone.

See what fills the phone

New in 0.5.0. A disk-usage scan shows folders sorted by size, a treemap, the biggest files and totals by type, so you can find what to clean up.

Real typing, from a real keyboard

Letters, accents, emoji, other languages and IME composition. Enter, Backspace, Delete, Tab, arrows, Home and End, plus Ctrl+A, C, V, X and Z.

Swipe typing on the phone

Slide across the letters and lift: a trail follows your finger, the best word goes in, and two more guesses wait above the keys. Backspace right after a swipe takes the whole word back.

Voice typing

Tap the mic and talk. Words show as you speak, then go in with sensible spacing and capitals. It prefers the phone's on-device speech recognizer, and you can make it on-device only.

Learns your words

Names and words you type often join a personal dictionary on the phone. Suggestions and a gentle, optional autocorrect help with tap-typing. Nothing is learned in password fields.

Paste from your PC

Got text on the computer already? Drop it in the Paste from PC box and send it to the phone in one go.

Optional mouse mode

A small pointer follows your PC mouse on the phone. Click to tap, hold to drag, scroll with the wheel, and use shortcuts for Back, Home and Recents.

Screen view (experimental)

See the phone's screen in the browser and click, drag and scroll right on it. Since 0.4.0 it's H.264 video from the phone's hardware encoder, smoother and lighter on Wi-Fi, with Auto, 720p and 1080p and a live fps and lag readout. Lock it to landscape or portrait while you work; your own rotation setting comes back afterwards. Since 0.5.0 a pointer on the phone follows your mouse over the picture, and the phone stays awake while you watch. Off until you turn it on, and Android asks every time.

Phone sound on the PC

Hear what apps play on the phone through the PC, with its own volume. The phone's own speaker goes quiet meanwhile and comes back to the same level afterwards. Apps that don't allow capture stay silent.

Notifications on the PC

Read the phone's notifications on the page, reply to messages and dismiss them. Off by default, only while connected, and never stored.

Files and clipboard, both ways

Drop files on the page to save them on the phone, or share from the phone to download them on the PC, each one checked end to end. Clipboard text goes both ways too.

Phone buttons

Volume, mute, Back, Home, Recents, the notification shade, quick settings, lock screen, screenshot and rotate, one click each.

Snippets, layouts and emoji

Save text you type often and insert it from the page, the keyboard or a shortcut. QWERTY, AZERTY, QWERTZ, Dvorak and Colemak layouts, and an emoji picker with search.

Quick, careful pairing

A one-time QR code that expires in about two minutes, a 6-digit code to compare on both screens, and Confirm on the phone. You can also type the code if the camera is a hassle.

Private by design

One computer at a time. Everything after pairing is encrypted and signed, and old messages can't be replayed. No internet use, no analytics and no accounts.

Only while you use it

The phone only listens while a session is running and the "session active" notification is showing (with a Stop button). It stops after the idle time you pick, from 5 minutes to 4 hours (30 minutes unless you change it), or you can keep it open until you stop it. Set it on the phone or the PC page.

A guide that walks you through it

A step-by-step setup guide with simple drawings, built into both the app and the web page, with directions for Pixel and Samsung phones.

Dark by default

The web page and the app come in dark and light themes, and the keyboard follows your phone unless you pick a theme.

Smooth scrolling

New in 0.5.0. The mouse wheel and touchpad scroll the phone in one smooth motion instead of small jumps.

Keeps up with the Wi-Fi

New in 0.4.0. The video adjusts its bitrate, and in Auto its size, to the connection, and the browser stays at the live edge instead of falling behind. If video isn't possible it switches to picture frames on its own.

Screenshots

Tap any screenshot to enlarge it. They're shown in dark mode, the default; “Light mode” under a screenshot opens the light version. All text, notifications and file names in them are made up, and the home screen behind the pointer and the phone screen in screen view are drawn stand-ins.

DeskLink is a personal project and isn't publicly available yet. Mouse mode uses Android's accessibility features only to perform taps and swipes you make with your PC mouse. It doesn't read what's on the screen. Voice typing uses the phone's speech recognizer; if the phone has no on-device recognizer, its speech service may process audio online. Screen view, notifications on the PC, the rotation lock and the file explorer (All files access) each need a permission you grant in Android's settings, and everything they send stays on the encrypted connection to the one paired computer.
